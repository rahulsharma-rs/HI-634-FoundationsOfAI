## Extracted chapter framework

The chapter's defensible workflow is decision-centered and proceeds through problem framing, target and prediction-time definition, dataset construction, correct-unit partitioning, pipeline-based preprocessing, baseline comparison, decision-relevant evaluation, interpretation and stress testing, a proceed-or-stop decision, and lifecycle documentation and monitoring.

The chapter distinguishes four learning paradigms:

- **Supervised:** labeled classification, regression, and ranking.
- **Unsupervised:** clustering, dimensionality reduction, and anomaly detection without supplied outcomes.
- **Semi-supervised:** a small labeled set plus a larger unlabeled set.
- **Self-supervised:** proxy tasks derived from raw data to learn representations.

The most important recurring cautions are:

- the model is only one component of a sociotechnical intervention;
- labels may be imperfect proxies shaped by documentation, access, or policy;
- prediction time determines which features are legitimate;
- preprocessing, selection, tuning, and resampling belong inside development-only pipelines;
- accuracy alone can conceal minority-class failure;
- discrimination, calibration, thresholds, workload, uncertainty, and subgroup performance answer different questions;
- coefficients, feature importance, clusters, anomaly scores, and SHAP values do not establish causality or clinical validity; and
- reproducibility, privacy, integrity, dependency security, monitoring, rollback, and retirement are part of the method.
